# Warehouse source of truth

Status: **binding implementation contract**  
Scope: MediStock Phoenix warehouse stock, dispatch, outlet receipt, and derived availability  
Baseline: migrations 060–064 remain immutable

## Decision

For stock supplied through the warehouse workflow, `warehouse_stock` is the
authoritative quantity and batch source. An outlet never recreates, edits, or
merges warehouse-origin stock through the manual availability editor.

A warehouse is a real warehouse entity. It is not modelled as a distribution
point. Movement between a warehouse and an outlet happens only through a
dispatch with an immutable, auditable lineage.

## Existing foundations

The following database foundations already exist and are not redefined here:

- migration 060: warehouse masters, batch-aware `warehouse_stock`, generated
  available quantity, and immutable `warehouse_stock_movements`;
- migration 061: dispatch headers and lines, outlet provenance identity,
  dispatch permissions, and one-outlet-movement-per-line idempotency;
- migration 062: durable warehouse/outlet assignments and scoped permission
  helpers;
- migrations 063–064: security hardening and identity-snapshot hotfix.

Historical migrations 001–064 must not be edited. Missing workflow functions
belong to migration 065 or later.

## Invariants

### Warehouse identity

- At most one active main warehouse exists per organization.
- A warehouse, stock row, dispatch, dispatch line, outlet, and assignment may
  never cross organization boundaries.
- Composite foreign keys are the structural enforcement mechanism. A UI check
  is never sufficient.

### Material identity and provenance

- Manufacturer batch number remains `NULL` when no manufacturer batch exists.
- Placeholder values such as `N/A`, `NONE`, `-`, and `بلا` are invalid.
- `has_no_batch_number = true` records an explicit no-batch answer.
- `internal_batch_reference` is private provenance. It distinguishes
  independently received no-batch stock and is never exposed through public QR.
- Warehouse-origin outlet rows carry the dispatch provenance reference.
- Migration 065 must add an explicit outlet `source_kind` discriminator with
  `manual` and `warehouse_dispatch` values. Existing rows start as `manual`.
- Every dispatch-origin outlet row receives a non-null private provenance key
  derived from its warehouse stock identity, including real-batch rows. This
  uses migration 061's existing eighth identity component to prevent a manual
  row and a warehouse-dispatch row from ever merging.
- The manual editor may only match `source_kind = 'manual'` rows with a null
  internal reference. It must never overwrite, reactivate, or apply quantity
  movements to a `source_kind = 'warehouse_dispatch'` row.

### Quantity truth

- `on_hand_quantity` is the physical warehouse quantity.
- `reserved_quantity` is stock committed to an in-flight dispatch.
- `available_quantity = on_hand_quantity - reserved_quantity` is generated by
  PostgreSQL and is never supplied by a client.
- Quantities are never negative and reserved quantity never exceeds on-hand.
- Every warehouse quantity or reservation change creates exactly one immutable
  movement row with before, delta, after, actor snapshot, and reason/reference.
- Movement history is append-only. Corrections are new movements, never edits.

### Dispatch lifecycle

Header states:

`draft -> sent -> partially_accepted | accepted | rejected`

A draft may also become `cancelled`. A sent dispatch is resolved from the
states of its lines; it never returns to draft.

Line states:

`pending -> accepted | accepted_with_difference | rejected`

A draft line may become `cancelled` when its dispatch is cancelled before
sending.

Required behavior:

1. Creating or editing a draft does not change stock.
2. Sending validates every line and atomically reserves its sent quantity from
   the referenced warehouse stock row.
3. Cancelling a draft cancels its lines without a stock movement.
4. Cancelling a sent dispatch is prohibited. Resolution happens through outlet
   decisions so reservation history is never bypassed.
5. Accepting a line:
   - requires the assigned receiving outlet and explicit received quantity;
   - consumes the reserved warehouse quantity;
   - creates or updates the provenance-safe outlet availability row;
   - appends one warehouse movement and one outlet movement;
   - records result links on the line;
   - is idempotent and cannot be repeated.
6. Accepting with a difference requires a non-empty difference reason. The
   warehouse consumption is the sent quantity; the outlet increase is the
   received quantity. The difference remains explicit in the dispatch record and
   audit trail.
7. Rejecting a line requires a reason, releases its reservation, creates no
   outlet stock, and is idempotent.
8. The dispatch header status is derived transactionally from its line states.
   Clients cannot choose a terminal header status directly.

## Separation of duty

- `warehouse_officer` is the sending side: view, create, edit draft, send,
  cancel draft, and audit. It cannot accept or reject.
- `port_officer` is the receiving side: view, accept, reject, and audit. It
  cannot create, edit, send, or cancel.
- `institution_admin` may oversee both sides inside its organization.
- `super_admin` retains the platform bypass, with full audit.
- `viewer` is read-only.
- A sender must never self-accept a line, even when custom overrides accidentally
  grant both permission sets. Actor separation is checked by the workflow RPC,
  not only by role defaults.

All non-super-admin actions require both the exact permission key and an active
assignment to the target warehouse or outlet. Organization scope may only be
narrowed by an assignment; never widened.

## Required server boundary

Clients receive no direct INSERT, UPDATE, or DELETE grant on:

- `warehouse_stock`;
- `warehouse_stock_movements`;
- `warehouse_dispatches`;
- `warehouse_dispatch_lines`;
- `item_availability_movements`.

Mutation is exposed only through reviewed `SECURITY DEFINER` functions with a
pinned `search_path = public`, explicit authentication, active-profile checks,
organization and assignment checks, permission checks, row locks, arithmetic
guards, idempotency guards, and audit logging.

The reviewed migration 065+ sequence must provide the smallest coherent function set:

- warehouse stock receipt/adjustment;
- create dispatch draft;
- upsert/delete draft line;
- send dispatch;
- cancel draft dispatch;
- accept dispatch line;
- reject dispatch line;
- assign/revoke profile scope, if no equivalent reviewed function exists.

Function execute privileges are revoked from `PUBLIC` and `anon`; only the
minimum authenticated entry points are granted.

## Outlet availability and public QR

- Outlet availability is a derived receiving projection for warehouse-origin
  stock, not an alternative warehouse ledger.
- Manual/legacy outlet rows remain explicitly distinguishable and must not merge
  with dispatch-origin rows.
- Public QR continues to expose only the reviewed public payload.
- `internal_batch_reference`, dispatch IDs, source documents, prices, supply
  type, national code, actor identity, and internal notes remain private unless
  a later separately reviewed public contract explicitly allows a field.

## Alerts, broadcasts, and status reporting

Alerts, broadcasts, dashboards, and Status Center may read warehouse/outlet facts
and produce derived signals. They do not mutate quantity and do not become a
second inventory source.

No automatic transfer or silent stock reconciliation is allowed. A recommendation
or alert can lead a human to create a dispatch, but cannot create one itself.

## Failure and concurrency rules

- Functions lock affected stock, dispatch, and line rows before validating state.
- Validation and mutation occur in one transaction.
- A failed multi-line send changes nothing.
- A failed acceptance changes neither warehouse stock nor outlet availability.
- Retrying a completed line returns its existing result or a stable
  already-decided error; it never creates a second movement.
- Network failure is not interpreted as rejection or permission denial.
- Error responses contain stable tokens and no secret, SQL text, or personal data.

## Audit requirements

Every write records:

- actor ID and role snapshot;
- organization, warehouse/outlet, dispatch, line, and stock references as
  applicable;
- action and prior/new state;
- before/delta/after quantities;
- reason for correction, difference, rejection, cancellation, or revocation;
- timestamp.

Deleting a user may null an actor foreign key but must not remove the durable
business timestamp, decision, quantity, or audit snapshot.
